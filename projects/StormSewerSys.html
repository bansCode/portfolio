Storm Sewer System Design for Adamsville Development

CIV340 Municipal Engineering
Jan 2026 – May 2026
Co-contributor: Michael Hollander, Nedel Ellacer, Vanik Park, and Julio Requejo

As part of a team of five in CIV340 at the University of Toronto, I contributed to the complete design of a storm sewer system for a proposed 16.7-hectare residential development in Adamsville, Ontario, serving 245 single-family homes, two mixed-use residential blocks, a school, and a park. The system was designed to withstand a 100-year storm event, in accordance with Ontario municipal engineering standards for pipe diameter, slope, flow velocity, cover depth, and manhole spacing.

The design process involved applying the Rational Method to calculate peak stormwater runoff from each drainage sub-area using IDF rainfall data, and Manning's equation to size each pipe for adequate capacity and velocity. A Google Spreadsheet model was developed to automate these calculations manhole-by-manhole from upstream to downstream, iterating through diameter and slope selections until all hydraulic criteria were satisfied. My contributions focused on drafting and revising the final design summary and design checks sections, as well as major revisions to the hydraulic spreadsheet calculations. The final network consists of 37 pipes connecting 31 maintenance holes, with pipe diameters ranging from 300 mm to 1350 mm and a peak design flow of 3,131.7 L/s discharged at a single outlet.

The completed design met all required criteria with no violations. Flow velocities across all pipes ranged from 0.84 m/s to 2.63 m/s, satisfying both the self-cleaning minimum and the erosion maximum. All pipes maintained subcritical flow, and minimum cover depth and invert depth requirements were satisfied throughout the network. The natural elevation gradient of the site — dropping from 228.5 m to 216 m northwest to southeast — was leveraged to drive gravity flow toward the outlet.
