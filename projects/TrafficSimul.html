Microscopic Simulation & Signal Optimization

CIV332 Transportation II: Performance
Jan 2026 – May 2026
Co-contributor: Michael Hollander, Md Ahnaf Zafir, and Jonathan Tang

As part of a team of four in CIV332 at the University of Toronto, I contributed to a multi-stage traffic engineering project that used Aimsun — a professional microscopic traffic simulation software — to model, analyze, and optimize real-world traffic conditions along the QEW highway corridor and a signalized intersection in the Mississauga area.

My specific contributions focused on traffic signal design and simulation setup. I configured the Aimsun dynamic scenario parameters including statistics intervals, warm-up periods, and traffic assignment settings, then extracted critical lane flows for each signal phase from the simulation output. Using Webster's method, I calculated optimal cycle lengths, effective and actual green times per phase, and yellow clearance intervals. I also performed a pedestrian movement check to verify that signal timings satisfied minimum crossing time requirements, adjusting the WE-T phase green time accordingly to ensure pedestrian safety compliance.

The project also involved analyzing the impact of a highway incident on traffic delay and route diversion along the QEW and South Sheridan Way, as well as evaluating the effectiveness of ramp metering at two on-ramps using flow-density fundamental diagrams and exit flow data. Results showed that ramp metering at the west on-ramp — identified as the primary bottleneck — significantly reduced mean density from 95.9 to 37.8 veh/km and increased exit-based flow by approximately 9%, demonstrating the practical benefits of active traffic management strategies.
